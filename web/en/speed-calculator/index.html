How to use the speed calculator

  1. Choose what you want to find: Find speed, Find distance or Find time. The group for that value is greyed out because the tool will fill it in.
  2. Enter the two values you know. Pick the unit that matches your data, for example miles and mph on a US road trip, or kilometers and km/h for a European route.

  5. Copy the summary with one click, or press Clear to try another trip.

The speed, distance and time formulas

Speed describes how quickly something covers distance. The relationship between the three quantities is one of the first formulas taught in physics, and it is easy to rearrange:

  • Speed = distance ÷ time, for example 150 miles in 3 hours is 50 mph.
  • Distance = speed × time, for example 65 mph for 2 hours covers 130 miles.
  • Time = distance ÷ speed, for example 240 miles at 60 mph takes 4 hours.

A handy way to remember them is the speed-distance-time triangle: cover the quantity you want and the other two show how to combine them. The units must be consistent. If your speed is in miles per hour, distance has to be in miles and time in hours. This calculator handles the conversions for you by turning everything into meters and seconds internally, then presenting the answer in familiar units.

Average speed versus instantaneous speed

The result you get here is an average speed: total distance divided by total time, including any slow stretches, traffic lights and breaks you choose to count. The number on a car speedometer is instantaneous speed, the value at one moment. If you drive 30 miles at 60 mph and then 30 miles at 30 mph, your average is not 45 mph. The trip takes 30 minutes plus 60 minutes, so 60 miles in 1.5 hours gives 40 mph. Averaging the two speeds directly overstates the result because you spent more time at the slower pace. For this reason, always add up total distance and total time first, then divide.

The key factors are 1 mile = 1.609344 kilometers, 1 knot = 1 nautical mile per hour = 1.852 km/h, and 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h. To go from mph to km/h multiply by 1.609; from km/h to mph multiply by 0.6214. Aviation and sailing use knots, while science and engineering prefer meters per second.

Running pace per mile and per kilometer

Runners and cyclists usually think in pace rather than speed: how many minutes it takes to cover one mile or one kilometer. Pace is the reciprocal of speed, so 6 mph equals a 10-minute mile, and 10 km/h equals a 6-minute kilometer. The Pace row shows both versions for every calculation. For a marathon of 26.2 miles, a 9:00 per-mile pace finishes in about 3 hours 55 minutes, while 8:00 per mile finishes in about 3 hours 30 minutes. To plan a race, enter the distance as 26.2 miles, enter your target finish time, and read the pace you must hold. The same approach works for a 5K (3.107 miles) or a half marathon (13.109 miles).

Practical examples

Road trip. You need to drive 420 miles and expect to average 60 mph. Choose Find time, enter 60 mph and 420 miles, and the answer is 7 hours. Add stops for fuel and meals on top of that.

Flight and sailing. A boat holding 12 knots covers 12 nautical miles in an hour, which is 13.8 miles. Use knots and nautical miles together for navigation, then read the statute-mile equivalent in the results.

Physics homework. A cyclist travels 500 meters in 40 seconds. Enter 500 in meters, 40 in seconds, and the tool returns 12.5 m/s, which is 45 km/h or about 27.96 mph.

If you also need to convert units outside speed, such as pounds to kilograms, try the Unit Converter. For hours that run past midnight, the Hours Calculator adds and subtracts clock times.

Tips for accurate results

  • Count total elapsed time, including stops, if you want the average speed of the whole trip. Count only moving time if you want the speed while moving.
  • Use the same distance source for every leg. Road distances and straight-line distances can differ by 20 percent or more.
  • Remember that speed limits and traffic change your real arrival time; treat the result as an estimate.
  • For very short times, use seconds. For multi-day journeys the time row switches to days, hours and minutes automatically.

Accuracy and limits

The calculator uses standard double-precision numbers and shows up to four decimal places. Values must be zero or positive; a time of zero cannot be used to find speed, and a speed of zero cannot be used to find time. Results are mathematical averages and do not take elevation, acceleration or weather into account.

Private by design

All calculations run locally in your browser. The numbers you enter are never uploaded or stored, no account is needed, and the page keeps working offline once loaded.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate speed?

Divide the distance by the time. If you travel 150 miles in 3 hours, speed is 150 ÷ 3 = 50 mph. In this calculator choose Find speed, enter the distance and the time, and the tool does the division and converts the result into several units.

How do I find distance from speed and time?

Multiply speed by time, making sure the units match. Driving at 65 mph for 2.5 hours covers 162.5 miles. Choose Find distance, enter 65 mph and 2 hours 30 minutes, and the tool returns the distance in miles, kilometers, meters and feet.

How do I find travel time?

Divide distance by speed. A 300 mile trip at 60 mph takes 5 hours. Select Find time, enter the speed and distance, and the result is displayed as hours, minutes and seconds along with decimal hours.

How do I convert mph to km/h?

Multiply miles per hour by 1.609344. For example 60 mph is about 96.56 km/h. The result panel always lists the main speed in both mph and km/h so you do not need a separate conversion.

Is the answer an average or an exact speed?

It is an average over the whole distance and time you entered. The speedometer shows instantaneous speed, which changes from moment to moment. For a trip with stops, include the stops in the time to get the average speed for the entire journey.

What is a knot?

A knot is one nautical mile per hour, equal to 1.852 km/h or about 1.151 mph. Ships and aircraft use knots. Select knots and nautical miles to work in those units directly.

How do I work out my running pace?

Choose Find speed, enter your distance and finish time, and read the Pace row, which shows minutes and seconds per mile and per kilometer. For example 5 kilometers in 25 minutes is a 5:00 per km pace, or 8:03 per mile.

Why can I not leave the time at zero?

Dividing by zero is undefined, so a trip that takes no time has no speed. Enter at least some seconds. Likewise a speed of zero cannot be used to find time because the distance would never be covered.

Are my numbers uploaded anywhere?

No. Every calculation happens in your browser. Nothing is sent, stored or tracked.
