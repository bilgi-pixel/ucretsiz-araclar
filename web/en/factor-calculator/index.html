How to use the factor calculator

  1. Type a positive whole number in the box. Commas and spaces are accepted as thousands separators, so 1,200 and 1200 mean the same thing.
  2. Press Find factors. The headline tells you how many factors the number has, and the list at the bottom shows each one in ascending order.
  3. Read the summary rows: the total count, the sum of all factors, the sum of the proper factors, and the prime factorization written with exponents.
  4. Check the number type. The tool tells you if the number is prime or composite, and whether it is perfect, abundant or deficient.
  5. Use Copy result to paste the factor list into homework, a spreadsheet or a message, or Clear to try another number.

What is a factor?

A factor of a number is a whole number that divides it exactly, leaving no remainder. The factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12, because each of them goes into 12 evenly. Factors always come in pairs that multiply to the original number: 1 × 12, 2 × 6 and 3 × 4. Every positive whole number has at least two factors, 1 and itself, except 1 which has just one. The words "factor" and "divisor" mean the same thing in this context, so a search for divisors of a number gives the same list.

Factors are not the same as multiples. Multiples of 12 are 12, 24, 36 and so on, going upward forever, while factors are the finite set of numbers below or equal to 12 that divide it. A handy memory aid is that factors are always less than or equal to the number, and multiples are always greater than or equal to it.

Prime and composite numbers

A prime number has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 and 13 are primes, and 2 is the only even one. A composite number has more than two factors, such as 12 or 100. The number 1 is neither prime nor composite, and the tool labels it as a special case. If you enter a prime, the factor list has just two entries and the prime factorization is the number itself.

Every composite number can be written as a product of primes in exactly one way, ignoring order. This is the fundamental theorem of arithmetic. For 360 the prime factorization is 23 × 32 × 5, and from the exponents you can predict how many factors exist: add one to each exponent and multiply, which gives (3+1) × (2+1) × (1+1) = 24 factors. The calculator confirms this count.

Perfect, abundant and deficient numbers

Ancient Greek mathematicians classified numbers by comparing them with the sum of their proper factors, meaning all factors except the number itself. If the sum equals the number, it is perfect. The first perfect numbers are 6 (1 + 2 + 3), 28, 496 and 8,128. If the sum is greater, the number is abundant: 12 has proper factors adding to 16. If the sum is smaller, the number is deficient, which is true of all primes and most numbers. The tool shows the proper factor sum and the classification so you can see the reasoning.

Where you will use factors

  • Simplifying fractions: find the largest factor shared by numerator and denominator to reduce 18/24 to 3/4.
  • Greatest common factor and least common multiple: list the factors of two numbers and pick the largest one they share.
  • Arranging things in equal groups: 36 chairs can form 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18 or 36 equal rows, which a factor list shows instantly.
  • Area and dimensions: the whole-number side lengths of a rectangle with area 48 are the factor pairs of 48.
  • Algebra: factoring numbers is the first step in factoring polynomials and solving quadratic equations.
  • Puzzles and coding: factor counts, divisor sums and perfect numbers appear in competitive programming and number theory problems.

Worked example: 360

Enter 360 and you will see 24 factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15, 18, 20, 24, 30, 36, 40, 45, 60, 72, 90, 120, 180 and 360. Their sum is 1,170, so the proper factors add to 810, which is more than 360 and makes it abundant. The prime factorization is 23 × 32 × 5. This is also why 360 is used for the degrees in a circle: it is divisible by so many numbers that halves, thirds, quarters, fifths, sixths, eighths and tenths of a turn are all whole degrees.

How big a number can I use?

The tool accepts whole numbers up to 1,000,000,000,000 (one trillion). It finds factors by trial division up to the square root of the number, which takes at most a million steps, so even the largest input finishes almost immediately. Perfect squares such as 144 have an odd number of factors, because the square root pairs with itself. Very large numbers with many prime factors can have hundreds or thousands of factors, and the list wraps neatly so it stays readable on a phone.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find all the factors of a number?

Test each whole number from 1 up to the square root of the number, and every time it divides evenly, record it and its partner. For 36 the pairs are 1 and 36, 2 and 18, 3 and 12, 4 and 9, and 6. The calculator does this automatically and lists the result in order.

What are the factors of 12?

The factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12. They form three pairs that multiply to 12: 1 × 12, 2 × 6 and 3 × 4. Enter 12 in the calculator to see them with the sum and the prime factorization.

What is the difference between factors and multiples?

Factors divide a number evenly and are never larger than it. Multiples are what you get by multiplying the number by 1, 2, 3 and so on, so they are never smaller than it. The factors of 6 are 1, 2, 3 and 6, while its multiples are 6, 12, 18 and onward.

How can I tell if a number is prime?

A prime number has exactly two factors, 1 and itself. Enter the number and look at the number of factors: if it is 2, the number is prime. The Number type row also states it directly.

What is a perfect number?

A perfect number equals the sum of its proper factors, the factors other than itself. The smallest is 6 = 1 + 2 + 3, followed by 28, 496 and 8,128. The calculator checks this and labels the number as perfect, abundant or deficient.

Why do perfect squares have an odd number of factors?

Factors normally come in pairs, but for a perfect square one factor is its own partner, because the square root times itself gives the number. For example 36 has nine factors, and 6 pairs with itself.

How do I find the greatest common factor with this tool?

Run the tool for both numbers and look for the largest value that appears in both lists. For 24 and 36 the shared factors are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12, so the greatest common factor is 12.

Is my number uploaded or saved?

No. The calculation happens in your browser only. Nothing is sent, stored or tracked.
