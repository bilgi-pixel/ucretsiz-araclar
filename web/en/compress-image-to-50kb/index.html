    2. Choose the target. The 50 KB button is selected by default; 20, 100 and 200 KB are one tap away, and the custom box accepts any whole number from 5 upward. Leave the "reduce the pixel dimensions" box ticked unless you must keep the exact original width and height.

    Why file size limits exist

    Anywhere that collects pictures from thousands of people has a reason to cap file size. Job portals, university applications, government and utility forms, e-commerce seller dashboards and online profiles all store and display every upload, and small files keep their systems fast and cheap. Limits such as 50 KB or 100 KB are common for passport-style headshots, signature scans and avatar images, while 200 KB to 1 MB is typical for product photos. The exact number is set by each organization and can change, so always read the instructions on the form you are filling in.

    The challenge is that a modern phone photo is enormous by comparison. A single picture from a recent phone is often three to six megabytes, which is sixty to a hundred times larger than a 50 KB limit. Getting from one to the other means throwing away a lot of information, and the trick is to throw away the part the eye notices least. That is what JPG compression does, and it is what this tool automates so you do not have to guess at quality settings.

    How the tool reaches your target size

    Compressing to a number of kilobytes is harder than it sounds because the size depends on the content of the picture. A smooth studio portrait compresses much further than a street scene full of leaves and signs, so a fixed quality setting gives wildly different sizes. Instead, this tool uses a search. It encodes the image at the lowest acceptable quality to see whether the target is reachable at all, then repeatedly tries a quality halfway between a size that fits and one that does not. After about seven rounds it has found the highest quality that stays under the limit, which is the sharpest picture your size budget allows.

    When even the lowest quality is too large, the tool falls back on shrinking the pixel dimensions step by step, since file size is roughly proportional to the number of pixels. It never drops quality below a sensible floor to avoid ugly blocks, preferring a smaller but cleaner picture. Because all of this runs on your own device, even a batch of photos finishes quickly and nothing is transmitted over the internet.

    Tips for a better small photo

    • Crop first. Removing empty background lets the same number of kilobytes cover only what matters, so the subject stays sharper.
    • Start from the original. A photo that has already been squeezed by a messaging app has less detail to work with. Use the camera file when you can.
    • Keep dimensions in mind. Some forms specify exact pixel sizes as well as a limit. Compress here first, then use the Resize Image tool to set the exact width and height, and check the size again afterwards.
    • Avoid repeated compression. Each round of JPG compression adds small errors. Always work from the original file instead of compressing an already compressed copy.
    • Check the preview. The thumbnail in the list switches to the compressed version after processing, so you can judge the quality before downloading.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I compress a photo to under 50 KB?

    Add the photo, keep the 50 KB button selected and press the button. The tool tests different JPG qualities until it finds the best one that stays under the limit, and reduces the pixel dimensions only if quality alone cannot get there.

    Can I choose a size other than 50 KB?

    Yes. Use the quick buttons for 20, 50, 100 or 200 KB, or type any value of 5 KB or more in the custom box. The result's file name includes the target, for example photo-50kb.jpg.

    Will the picture look blurry after compressing to 50 KB?

    Some loss of detail is unavoidable, but a typical portrait stays clearly readable at 50 KB. Busy photos with fine texture suffer most. If the result looks too soft, raise the target or crop away empty background first.

    What does the option to reduce pixel dimensions do?

    If lowering JPG quality alone cannot reach the target, the tool also shrinks the picture a little at a time and tries again. Turn the option off to keep the original dimensions, but small targets may then fail.

    Is the output always a JPG?

    Yes. JPG gives the smallest files for photographs. PNG and WebP inputs are converted, and any transparent area is filled with white because JPG cannot store transparency.

    Is it safe to compress passport or ID photos here?

    The images are processed entirely inside your browser and are never uploaded. Always check the requirements of the organization you are applying to, since they may specify exact dimensions as well as a size limit.

    Can I compress several photos at once?

    Yes. Add as many photos as you like; each is brought under the same limit, and you can download them one by one or as a single zip.

    Why could a photo fail to reach the target?

    Extremely small targets such as 5 or 10 KB are hard for large, detailed pictures, and with the dimension option off the tool cannot shrink the image. The page lists the files that failed and suggests what to change.

    What about HEIC photos from an iPhone?

    Browsers cannot open HEIC files, so the tool skips them with a message. Change Camera Formats to Most Compatible on the iPhone, or export the photo as JPG from the Photos app.
